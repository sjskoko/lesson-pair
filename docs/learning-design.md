# Learning design and what to measure

LessonPair helps a learner explain a source in their own words, practice a small gap, and try again. It is an implementation of familiar learning ideas, not a claim to have invented a new scientific method.

- **Attempt before explanation:** exposes what the learner can already express and where help is useful.
- **Focused feedback:** keeps the task small, with a cue, starter, or labeled example when needed.
- **Learner-written retry:** supplies evidence from the learner. A model's rewrite cannot serve as that evidence.
- **Later, new-context recall:** checks whether an expression can be used beyond repeating the original sentence.

Research on retrieval practice and distributed practice motivates this design, but does not validate this specific product or prove language gains for every learner. See [Roediger and Karpicke (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x) and [Dunlosky et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266). Feedback quality and source difficulty depend on the model and material.

## Small pilot proposal

Start with consenting adult learners and public or licensed source excerpts. Collect only what is needed, keep records private, and allow withdrawal. Compare the guided workflow with an equal-duration video-and-summary activity. Use counterbalanced topics or random assignment; do not compare an AI rewrite against a learner baseline.

Measure completion, time to first learner answer, help requested, and whether learners return. For learning, collect a delayed explanation and a new-context expression task without hints; have a blinded rater assess meaning and appropriate expression use with an agreed rubric. Record assistance and missing answers. Report sample size, uncertainty, and dropouts, not just the best examples.

Until such a study is conducted, say “designed to support practice,” not “proven to improve fluency.” GitHub stars, enjoyable demos, and improved sample sentences are not measures of durable learning.
